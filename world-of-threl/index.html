Project Threl · Setting guide

The World of Threl

Threl is a grimdark theological tabletop role-playing setting where reality no longer holds itself together by nature. Dabar, memory, resonance, and the daily work of ordinary people keep the remaining world from falling into the Fray.

A world after the breaking

The Great Decoherence

The Grand Monochord once represented a single, sustaining harmony. When it shattered during the Great Decoherence, the relationship between language, meaning, and material existence fractured with it. The world did not simply become dangerous; it became dependent upon maintenance.

A mountain may still stand and a fire may still burn, but neither can be taken entirely for granted. The people of Threl preserve what remains through work, memory, ritual, and the careful use of the Word.

The world has not ended. It has become a thing that must be kept.

Where reality holds and fails

Anchors, the Fray, and the Lattice

The Anchors

Scriptorium-Cities, commonly called Anchors, are maintained zones of relative stability. Chant-clergy, artisans, healers, scholars, and ordinary citizens all contribute to the work that lets gravity, water, fire, memory, and community remain dependable.

The Fray

Beyond the last dependable light lies the Fray: unspooled reality where definitions weaken and separate things may bleed into one another. It is not a sentient demon or a conventional hell. It is the wound left when the world forgets itself.

The Lattice

The Lattice is the stabilizing structure through which matter, meaning, memory, and resonance remain connected. When its maintenance fails, the consequences appear as drift, distortion, Scarred Nodes, and Green Rot.

Scarred Nodes

Where reality failed most violently, persistent wounds remain. Scarred Nodes draw instability toward them and may produce impossible echoes, altered landscapes, or the definition-eating growth known as Green Rot.

The Word beneath the world

Dabar

Dabar is a disciplined language and interface for influencing the deeper harmonics of reality. Practitioners do not own creation or invent arbitrary magic. They recover bounded fragments of the Word and speak them with consequence.

A valid Phrase can preserve a boundary, animate a process, reveal what is hidden, or alter a condition. Reality resists careless speech, so every use of Dabar carries responsibility, cost, and the possibility of lasting change.

Three necessary, incomplete voices

The Three Covenants

The Father, Son, and Spirit are the three aspects of the Triune: principles that structure reality rather than unrelated gods. Each is necessary. Each is incomplete. The tension between them is part of the world’s continuing conversation.

The Father

Law, structure, stasis, and judgment. The Father gives the world a form that can endure, but taken alone, that stability may harden into petrification.

The Son

Grace, virtue, action, healing, and fluidity. The Son gives living movement to what has been established, but action without restraint may become instability.

The Spirit

Mystery, transition, prophecy, and the liminal. The Spirit reveals what fixed structures conceal, but insight without structure or action may dissolve into uncertainty.

The Unchained (Collective)

The Unchained (Collective) stands as a secular alternative to the Triune Church. Its members question inherited authority and seek their own answers without erasing the dangers of the world they inhabit.
